## 1. Method and stated limitations

Research was performed live against current search results rather than from memory or from 2024/2025 assumptions.

**What was used**

- Brave Search via direct fetch, which served live 2026 result pages during the research window.
- Bing via direct fetch for cross-checking query variations.
- Direct fetch of publisher pages for source verification (Business Standard, MAK Buildings, Metal Tree, Tata Nexarc).
- Government and official-industry sources where available (Invest Odisha / IPICOL, Department of Steel and Mines, Government of Odisha).

**Limitations that must be stated plainly**

- The primary web-search tool reported its provider unavailable during the research window. Results were therefore obtained by fetching search engines directly. This returns a live result set but not a full ranking dataset.
- **Exact search volume could not be independently verified for any term.** No keyword tool with verifiable monthly volume data was reachable. No monthly search-volume number appears anywhere in the published article or in this document.
- Google Trends data could not be retrieved (the interface requires JavaScript execution that fetch-based access did not provide). Trend statements below are therefore inferred from publication recency and publication volume across competitors, not from Trends.
- Competitor publication dates are as reported by the publishers themselves or by the search index, and are reproduced with their source.

## 2. Live search landscape

### 2.1 Observed demand pattern

Query families examined: PEB, PEB structure, PEB cost, PEB cost per sq ft, industrial shed, industrial shed cost, steel shed, warehouse construction, warehouse cost, structural steel fabrication, structural steel erection, PEB manufacturer, PEB contractor, PEB construction, pre-engineered building, steel building, factory shed, industrial building, roofing and cladding, industrial maintenance, mechanical erection — each also combined with India, Odisha, Sambalpur, Bhubaneswar, Hirakud, Jharsuguda, Angul, Jajpur, Rourkela and Cuttack.

Observation:

- **Cost and pricing queries dominate commercial intent.** A single fetch for "PEB cost per sq ft India 2026" returned a first page consisting almost entirely of cost guides, cost calculators and price breakdowns, all published or updated in 2026. Bing reported approximately 69,300 results for that phrasing.
- **The "cost per sq ft" family is actively contested.** At least ten distinct publishers currently hold first-page positions for PEB/industrial-shed cost phrasings, most with 2026 publication or update dates.
- **Location-modified demand is real but thin in supply.** Queries combining Odisha cities with PEB terms returned directory listings and pan-India lead-generation pages rather than technical local content.
- **No meaningful demand was observed for the Sambalpur/Hirakud/Cuttack long-tail variants** beyond directory pages. These were therefore not pursued as primary targets, and are not stuffed into the article.

### 2.2 Trend signals (inferred, not from Trends)

Signals that this topic is currently active rather than stale:

- Fresh 2026 publication dates across nearly every ranking competitor, several within four weeks of the research date.
- Multiple publishers maintaining dedicated cost-calculator assets, which indicates sustained commercial demand rather than a one-off news spike.
- Odisha-specific investment activity covered by national business press through September 2026 (see §6), which lifts the relevance of industrial construction topics in this geography.
- Cost-query content is durable: it does not expire with a news cycle, and it can be updated in place as prices move.

### 3.1 Content gap identified

**The published price figures do not reconcile, and nobody explains why.**

MAK Buildings quotes roughly ₹350–550 per sq ft for a "basic PEB structure". Kishore Industries, in the same result set, quotes ₹1,000–2,500 per sq ft. Aajjo directory listings show ₹120–185 per sq ft. These are not competing estimates of the same thing — they are prices for structurally different scopes:

- fabricated steel structure only
- structure supplied and erected
- a completed turnkey building including civil works, flooring and services
- individual components or per-unit items

**Not one reviewed page states a scope boundary directly beside its headline figure in a way a buyer can act on.** Several say "depending on scope" in passing, then quote a single band. The result is that a buyer comparing ₹350 with ₹2,500 has no way to tell whether they are being offered a bargain or a steel frame with no roof.

### 3.2 Other gaps observed

- **No published Odisha/Eastern India specifics.** Metal Tree references "coastal and eastern India" once, in relation to soil. No reviewed page addresses interior-road transport constraints, the June–September monsoon window as a cladding constraint, or permit-bound working inside operating plants in the Sambalpur–Jharsuguda–Angul–Rourkela belt.
- **No scope-comparison instrument.** No reviewed page gives a buyer a matrix to send to bidders.
- **No steel-price-basis guidance.** None explains why a per-square-foot rate needs a validity period and a stated steel price assumption.
- **Weak or unattributed sourcing.** Most pages cite no source and no date for their figures.
- **Directory saturation in local results.** Odisha-specific PEB queries return Justdial / IndiaMART / TradeIndia / Aajjo listings rather than technical content — a genuine local content vacuum.

### 3.3 What Google appears to reward in this set

- A direct answer near the top (several winners put a short answer or key-takeaways block above the fold)
- FAQ blocks with conversational question headings
- Tables comparing options or cost bands
- Recent visible dates
- Regional specificity, where provided (the Metaltree page's regional banding is the clearest example)

## 5. Recommended topic and rationale

### Selected

**"PEB cost per sq ft in India (2026): what the price covers"**
Published at `/blog/peb-cost-per-sq-ft-india`.

### Why this one

1. **Highest commercial intent in the set.** Cost queries are placed by buyers, not browsers. Every competitor in the space is producing cost content, which is itself evidence of commercial value.
2. **Largest defensible content gap.** The ₹350-to-₹2,500 discrepancy is visible on a single search results page and is unexplained by every publisher in the set. Reconciling it is genuinely useful, cannot be answered by a competitor's existing page, and does not require inventing a price.
3. **It can be written without fabricating anything.** The article's core is scope structure and cost drivers — engineering and procurement reasoning — rather than proprietary price data AKEN would have to invent.
4. **It converts.** A reader who understands the scope matrix has a concrete next step, and the article naturally routes them to a scope review.
5. **It is durable.** Steel prices move and one paragraph can be updated; the scope analysis does not expire.
6. **It carries the Odisha angle honestly.** The local section is built on sourced 2026 investment activity rather than on keyword insertion.
7. **It is honest about what cannot be claimed.** Publishing "there is no single defensible market rate, and here is why" is a stronger E-E-A-T signal than a rival number.

### Why not the others

- PEB vs RCC already exists on the AKEN blog and on competitor sites; it is better used as an internal link target here.
- Erection sequence, fabrication quality and cladding specification remain strong follow-ups and are natural next articles; they have lower immediate commercial intent than cost.
- "Trends 2026" content ages fastest and carries the highest risk of unsupported claims.

### Explicitly not claimed

No ranking outcome is predicted or promised. This is a topic-selection recommendation based on observed competitor activity, observed intent, and an identified content gap — not a guarantee of search position.
